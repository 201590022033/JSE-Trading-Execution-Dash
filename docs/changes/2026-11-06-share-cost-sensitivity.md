# Share cost estimates

Two Standard Bank public pages disagree: the detailed OST fee page lists 0.5% brokerage and an R110 minimum before VAT, while the current product page displays 0.25% brokerage. Neither page establishes the owner's individual or negotiated rate; neither supplies evidence that the 0.5% page's minimum applies to the 0.25% offer. The calculator presents both scenarios, explicitly treating the R110 minimum as an assumption for the 0.25% scenario. Sources: https://onlinesharetrading.standardbank.co.za/standimg/OST/fees-and-costs.html and https://www.standardbank.co.za/southafrica/personal/products-and-services/grow-your-money/share-trading/online-share-trading-account (accessed 6 November 2026).

On 6 November the owner-authorized, signed-in OST account cost-structure screen showed 0.50% equity brokerage with an R110 minimum, excluding VAT, for the selected account. This verifies that account screen's displayed brokerage terms at that time, not the exact settlement levies, rounded invoice or a different account's terms. The same screen separately listed CFD brokerage and funding-related rates; the cash-share calculator must not be used as a CFD estimate.

The calculator reuses `published_share_fee`: brokerage, STRATE, investor protection levy, assumed 15% VAT and 0.25% buy-side share purchase tax, with assumed component rounding. Optional spread and slippage are specified in basis points for the round trip. It estimates cash shares only, not ETFs, tax-free accounts, CFDs, SSFs or historic tariffs. It excludes ongoing account fees, tax on gains and unentered spread and slippage. The 2010 trade notes predate the available OST contract-note selector, which starts in December 2022; actual account charges remain unverified.

The BTI and SASOL target-design API adds a closed-trade sensitivity for both brokerage scenarios. It applies the illustrative fee schedule to each existing fill and quantity while retaining each existing execution-price impact. This is not a new portfolio replay: it cannot show how different fees would have changed position sizes, cash, later fills, drawdowns or strategy choice. Existing frozen reports and model settings are unchanged. Generic historical target-study fees remain visible beside the scenario.

Example: 100 shares bought at R100 and sold at R105, no spread or slippage: gross R500; illustrative buy fees R158.76, sell fees R133.76, net R207.48, break-even R102.93. Both brokerage scenarios produce the same estimate at this size because both assume the R110 minimum. These are calculations, not account quotations.

The separate CFD calculator defaults to R57.50 brokerage including VAT on entry and exit, based on the observed ABSA CFD entry and the owner's statement that the exit costs the same. That is an assumption for estimates, not a verified future exit invoice. It assumes one underlying share per contract, supports long and short, and takes financing and other charges as explicit inputs. Fill prices must already include applicable spread; the calculator does not separately add the displayed market-maker rate. It does not apply cash-share transaction tax. No order, account setting or stored broker position is changed.
